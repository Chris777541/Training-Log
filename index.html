Estimates

Your calories

With your height, resting calories use the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which studies have found to be the most accurate of the common equations for most adults (within 10% for about 4 in 5 people). Without height, they use the Schofield equations (WHO/FAO), which need only age, sex and weight. Daily calories multiply that by an activity factor within the FAO's ranges for sedentary, active and vigorous lifestyles. Workout calories multiply your resting rate by activity values (METs) from the Compendium of Physical Activities: 6.0 for heavy lifting (6 reps or fewer), 3.5 for lighter lifting (8–15 reps), and the matching value for each kind of cardio.
